## 1. CONCEPT LOCK

**"Only One Thing Left The Vault"** — 42 seconds, no dialogue.

A thief breaks into the most secure vault in the city, where a single bottle of NOVA sits under a shaft of light. He opens an empty briefcase beside it, and the audience assumes the bottle is going in. Instead he lifts it, sprays it once on his wrist, and **puts it back**. He closes the empty case and walks out.

On the roof the guards corner him. He doesn't run. He slides the case across the wet concrete to them and steps back into the rain. The collector, Solen, opens the recovered case under a gold lamp. There is only an empty bottle-shaped cavity in the velvet. Cut to the vault: the bottle is still standing on the plinth. Nothing was stolen.

Cut to the gala upstairs, still going as if nothing happened. Kade walks through the crowd in a wet overcoat. Vero, the guest who couldn't take her eyes off the bottle earlier, passes him, stops and turns back. She recognises the scent.

> **They took the case. He took NOVA.**

**The logic, for anyone who asks.** The case was always empty and was always the decoy. The alarm follows the case, so the chase follows the case. The only thing that left the vault is on his skin.

**Two signature devices:**

1. **Mist.** The film opens by pushing through a burst of atomizer mist (shot 02). It crosses into the gold world through mist (shot 11). Its single most important action is one spray on a wrist (shot 09A). It ends on the sound of one spray over black (shot 15). The mist is the transition, the plot and the product all at once.
2. **The star caustic.** The bottle's hollow core refracts light into an eight-armed star with a dark centre. It appears twice, and both times it comes from the bottle itself: on Kade's face the first time we see it (shot 07), and on the wall behind the hero bottle (shot 15). **It never appears without the bottle in frame.** That rule is what fixed the v1 wrist problem.

**Colour story.** Neon blue throughout. Gold only in Solen's room. Blue is the thief's world and gold is the money's world. When the case opens empty, gold holds for three seconds of silence, then the vault cut and the final walk return to blue.

Paste this verbatim at the end of **every** sheet prompt. It is the single biggest lever on whether these read as photography or as AI.

```

SKIN AND MATERIAL TRUTH — Visible open pores across the nose, cheeks and

symmetrical face, glowing eyes, HDR halo, over-sharpened edges, illustration,
digital painting, cartoon, anime, text, letters, numbers, watermark, logo,
signature, brand marks.
```

## 5. WORKFLOW RULES

Four rules that matter more than any individual prompt.

**1. Light the sheets flat and neutral.** No neon blue anywhere on a character or location sheet. If you bake the ad's lighting into a reference, every downstream shot inherits those shadows and you can never relight the face. Drama belongs in the shot prompts, never in the sheet.

**2. Generate wide, then lock, then never regenerate.** For each character, produce four to six variations of the **front view only**. Pick the one face you want. From that moment, that single image is `@image1` for every other angle, every wardrobe and every shot. Regenerating angle by angle from scratch is the number one cause of identity drift.

**3. Nothing is powered on a sheet.** No lasers firing, no LEDs lit, no glowing lenses, no illuminated panels. You want the object, not the effect. Light goes on in the shot prompts, where you can control it.

**4. No text is ever generated.** No serial numbers, no engraved names, no signage. Anything that must read as letters (NOVA, the tagline) is typed in After Effects. Generated lettering comes out garbled, and garbled text is the fastest way to look AI-made.
