## 1. How I scored

Five axes, 20 points each, as the panel asked:

- **CS cinematic storytelling**: one idea built through image, sound and timing; a real turn; a loop.
- **ET emotional truth**: a human truth a stranger nods at; no invented facts about Jawad after the reframe; and
  whether it still works with the Higgsfield preset voice "Vlad" (a warm narrator, not an intimate real voice).
- **OR originality**: how far it is from the first idea everyone has, and from his own earlier reels.
- **VS visual signature**: is there one frame that could stand for the whole piece, and could only Jawad make it.
- **BF brand fit**: flame on black, serif-italic keyword plus white grotesk, glowing underline, rim-lit JD, an
  editor's lens on a universal desi feeling.

Then a **production-risk deduction (0 to 12)** for today's reality: CPU-only Blender props, JD only as 2.5D cut-outs
from two sheets, procedural sound, about 18 min of render per 35 s reel per worker, five reels today, zero mistakes.
Premium-but-buildable beats ambitious-but-risky. I scored the **director's version** of each card (fixes in section
3); where the fix cannot rescue the idea, the score says so.

## 3. Director's Pass, candidate by candidate

### C08 Ek Frame ki Keemat (83)
- **Core:** respect (awe that turns into "ab samjhe?").
- **Killed:** a VFX breakdown montage with a before/after wipe (every studio posts one); a time-lapse of an editor at
  a desk with a spinning clock; a rate card explaining why editing costs money.
- **POV:** the frame itself, the 1/30 of a second a thumb gives it before scrolling on.
- **Contradiction:** cathedral scale on 33 milliseconds; stillness inside a medium made of motion.
- **Human truth:** "Jo cheez aasaan dikhti hai, us ke peeche kisi ki raat hoti hai." Everyone has heard "bas 5 minute
  ka kaam hai" about their work.
- **Single image:** side-on, one paused frame pulled apart into a loaf of glowing glass slices; one slice holds JD's
  face, the slice beside it holds only his rim light, separated from him by a hand's width of darkness.
- **Truth fix:** the card's "main ne ise kai ghante diye" and the per-layer minutes ("rim light · 25 min") are
  invented. Replace them with numbers that are **true by construction**: the hook frame's real layer count read from
  the reel module (I plan 14), "1 second = 30 frames", and "is reel mein 1,024 frames" (16 bars at 112.5 BPM =
  34.133 s = exactly 1,024 frames at 30 fps).
- **Voice:** explanatory narrator; Vlad fits.
- **Build:** the lowest-risk reel in the pool. The layers are the toolkit's own draw calls cached as sprites and
  placed as planes. No Blender needed.
- **Verdict:** slate. The client send ("us client ko bhejo") is the most specific send in the pool.

### C15 Log Kya Kahenge (82)
- **Core:** fear, then relief.
- **Killed:** a man ringed by whispering shadow faces; chains snapping or a bird leaving a cage; a sunrise
  "ignore the haters" speech.
- **POV:** the compositor's eye. An editor knows how a flat card fakes depth, so he is the one person who can see the
  crowd is fake.
- **Contradiction:** a roaring stadium inside a private thought; the vast crowd is paper-thin.
- **Human truth:** "Jin logon ke darr se hum ruke hain, woh khud apne 'log kya kahenge' mein ruke hue hain." Everyone
  is someone else's "log".
- **Single image:** mid-orbit, the tiers turn edge-on into razor-thin lines of light; behind each line a row of real
  people sits heads-down, faces lit only by their own phones.
- **Director's addition:** one line that makes it Jawad's: "Main editor hoon. Flat cheez ko gehra dikhana mera kaam
  hai." It sets up the reveal with his craft (2.5D parallax is literally how this whole set puts his face into worlds).
- **Truth fix:** "Saalon tak main ne apni zindagi ... edit ki" implies a private history. Use "Hum saalon tak apni
  zindagi un logon ke liye edit karte hain, jo poori video dekhte bhi nahi."
- **Flashpoint fix:** an amphitheatre, never a cricket ground: no pitch, no wickets, no team colours (India beat
  Pakistan in the Asian Games final on 3 Oct; stadium imagery must not read as that).
- **Repeat check:** his "You made it" reel opened on spotlights clunking on. Here the hook is the head-snap with the
  floodlight already on; one clunk only, at the warm-light turn.
- **Build:** medium. Silhouettes are procedural (8 variants, front and turned), composited into one strip per row; the
  head-snap is a cached two-state blend swept by a mask; the orbit is `K.Cam` over 20-30 row planes. Test 3 stills first.
- **Verdict:** slate.

### C11 Bijli Chali Gayi (83)
- **Core:** nostalgic amusement, then quiet pride (sabr).
- **Killed:** a sepia flashback of a child doing homework by candle; a "90s kids yaad hai?" object montage; the UPS-beep
  meme with a reaction face.
- **POV:** the reel is a house on the grid. The video itself loses power.
- **Contradiction:** the darkest reel in the set is lit like luxury cinema; a power cut is the most beautiful light.
- **Human truth:** "Light jaati thi, toh saara ghar ek kamre mein aa jaata tha." And for editors: the finger that
  presses Ctrl+S by itself.
- **Single image:** black; a phone-torch beam slices through dust and finds the orange LED of a UPS blinking, a hand
  fan mid-swing, and the keyword *bijli* glowing faintly as the only thing still on battery.
- **Truth fix:** "Phir main ne editing shuru ki ... meri sab se bari dushman", "Tab se meri ungli" and "Bijli ne mujhe
  sikhaaya" imply his personal history. Use "Phir editing aayi. Aur bijli ban gayi sab se bari villain", "Tab se har
  editor ki ungli khud Ctrl+S dabati hai", "Bijli ne humein sabr sikhaaya".
- **Repeat check:** the card's "unsaved project" dialog is too close to his Yaadein ERROR / delete dialogs. Cut it: the
  laptop simply dies (screen collapses to a line, battery 0 % in RED).
- **Sound fix:** a synthesised crowd cheer ("aa gayi!") would sound fake. The power return is voiced by the appliances
  themselves (fan spinning up, CRT pop, fridge thunk) and the narrator says the line.
- **Flashpoint fix:** nostalgic only; no government or utility names, no load-shedding politics.
- **Build:** low-medium, and darkness is a budget dividend: a minimal room (wall, window grille, CRT, ceiling fan
  silhouette) revealed through a 2D beam mask; 5 static Blender props.
- **Verdict:** slate, opening reel (strongest scroll-stop in the pool).

## 4. New concept: N01 Ek Awaaz ("one sound won't render")

*Archetype* A13 editing reveal + A3 letter, inverted · *Emotion* wonder, then longing, then tenderness ·
*Look* `gold_hour` · *Length* about 34 s, beatless (VO-phrase grid) · *Send* "Yeh reel ghar bhejo"

**Logline.** Every sound of a desi morning in this reel is synthetic: the pressure cooker's whistle, the ceiling fan,
chai boiling over, the doorbell, the milkman's cycle bell. An editor can build a whole home out of nothing. Except one
track.

**Why it beats the cards it replaces.** It keeps C23's authority (the editor controls what you feel) and C10's send
(to family) and fixes both: muted viewers get the whole story from labels, meters and one dark channel; no private
fact is needed; and the synthetic narrator is honest inside a film about synthetic sound. The claim "ek bhi
recording nahi" is literally true of this production (procedural `audio.py` SFX, TTS narrator). Mosseri's 2026 test
was "something only you could create"; this reel proves its own premise as you watch it.

**Hook (0-2.5 s)**
- On screen: frame 0, a dark mixing console already mid-move; fader 1 slams up and the 3D pressure cooker on top of
  that channel blasts a jet of glowing steam; its meter spikes orange. Text by 0.5 s: `YEH AWAAZ / *asli* / NAHI HAI`.
- Heard: the cooker whistle on frame 0 (a sound every viewer in both countries knows before they see it), then the VO
  at 0.3 s: "Cooker ki yeh seeti... asli nahi hai."
- Withheld: which sound is real, and why an editor is faking a kitchen.

**Beats (VO-phrase grid; cuts and steam wipes 0.1-0.4 s after phrase ends)**

| t (s) | picture | VO draft (universal, for the hinglish-scriptwriter) / text |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0-2.5 | hook | as above |
| 2.5-9.5 | channels 2-6 rise one per phrase; each object lights itself only while its sound plays (fan, chai patila, doorbell chime, cycle bell, steel glass); each strip draws its live waveform from the real audio; mono labels `CH 02 · PANKHA` (40 px) | "Pankha. Chai ka ubaal. Doorbell. Doodhwale ki ghanti. Ek bhi recording nahi. Sab computer pe bana hai." |
| 9.5-15.0 | full mix: all faders up; gold morning light blooms out of the console (sound is light); lockup `POORA / *ghar*` | "Ek editor, ek computer pe poora ghar bana sakta hai. Har subah. Har awaaz." |
| 15.0-16.0 | the lateral dolly reaches the last strip; every channel mutes at once: 8 frames of true silence, then room tone | (silence) |
| 16.0-24.0 | CH 07 is dark. Its label, in `jw_body`: "khana lag gaya!". The fader rises by automation; the meter stays dead: `NO SIGNAL`. JD (suit, profile, rim-lit) looks at it. Text `YEH *awaaz* / BANTI NAHI` | "Bas ek track kabhi nahi banta. Jab ghar se koi awaaz deta hai... 'khana lag gaya!'" |
| 24.0-29.5 | one warm lamp returns; JD hand-on-chest; lockup `AAJ / *ghar* / CALL KARO` | "Ghar se door ho, toh aaj call kar lena. Woh awaaz sun lo." |
| 29.5-34.0 | end card over the living console: headline "Kuch awaazein render nahi hoti." CTA pill "Yeh reel ghar bhejo" | spoken CTA, then the loop: fader 1 starts to rise |

**World Bible (minimum set)**
- Palette: night NIGHT_0 #070404; object light FLAME #FF6A1A with AMBER #FFB547 cores; full-mix bloom GOLD #FF9F1C;
  CH 07 stays SMOKE #2A1A15 (never lit).
- Light logic: **sound is the only light.** An object glows only while its sound plays; silence is darkness.
- Lens set: 50 mm along the console (honesty), 100 mm macro on meters and fader caps, 85 mm on JD.
- Camera law: one slow lateral dolly, left to right (the morning moving forward), never reversing; it stops at CH 07.
- Texture: fine warm grain, halation on meters, a thin haze of steam in the air.
- Sound motif: the cooker whistle (opens and loops the reel).
- Symbol: the fader (up = presence).
- Forbidden: any synthesised human voice except the narrator (the call home is never voiced); real appliance brands;
  religious sounds (azaan, temple bells); photo frames or garlanded portraits (it is about distance, not death); sad
  piano cliche; cursor hesitation (a Yaadein device).

**Single image (anchor and cover):** the console in the dark, every channel glowing orange under its household object,
except the last one: dark, fader up, meter dead, label "khana lag gaya!".

**Sound:** no score until the payoff; the household sounds are the music. Under "aaj ghar call karo" one warm pad
chord (score.py, or `harmonium_swell` if built). The cooker whistle is the hero sound and the first build gate.
**Caption device:** waveform captions (playbook 4.8 #6): each channel's waveform draws its own words.
**Build:** console from `ui.py` glass strips laid in perspective; meters driven by the real SFX envelopes exported
by the sound-designer (picture and sound cannot drift); 5-6 static Blender props in `assets3d_jawad.py` (pressure
cooker, ceiling fan with a separate blade sprite rotated in 2D, chai patila, doorbell chime, cycle bell, steel glass)
at about 1-2 min CPU each; steam from `K.Particles`. JD crops: `suit_profile`, `suit_hand_on_chest`.
**Risks:** (1) the household sounds must convince: gate the cooker whistle in a blind listen first, and drop any
sound that fails rather than ship it; (2) a dark channel can read as bereavement: the VO says "ghar se door" and the
CTA is a phone call, and the Forbidden list bans death imagery; (3) it shares a "what is inside" structure with C08,
so post them apart (slots 3 and 5).
**Scores:** CS 17 · ET 19 · OR 18 · VS 16 · BF 17 = 87, risk -5, **final 82**.

## 6. Production reality: build order, gates and fallbacks

- **VO first, one session.** Five Devanagari takes on Vlad (about 2.5 credits per 520-character take; five reels plus
  retakes stay well inside the 187 credits left). Vlad measured 143 wpm raw; after the 1.06-1.10x stretch in
  `vo_config.json` plan about 2.6-2.75 words/s. Every VO draft above is within 70-85 words.
- **Gates (about 30-45 min total, before any full render):** N01 cooker whistle blind listen; C15 three crowd stills; C11
  one beam-mask still; C24 one second of the revolving divider; C08 one exploded-stack still. A gate that fails swaps
  the reel for its reserve: N01 to C02 (director's version), C15 to C01, C24 to C09, C11 or C08 have no realistic
  failure mode.
- **Render budget:** about 5,200 frames for the set. At 1-2 s per frame on one worker that is 1.5-3 h; use two workers
  under `nice -n 10` only when the box allows. Preview every reel at 1 sample with `--sheet` before the master.
- **Blender:** about 14 static props in total (C11 five, C24 two, N01 six, keycap one), CPU, `threads = 2`, low samples
  plus OIDN, 1-2 min each. No animated Blender camera renders anywhere in the slate.
- **Render order:** C08 first (lowest risk, proves the pipeline), then C11, C24, C15, N01.
- **Shared code:** end card, captions, `finish()` exposure-push glue and the colorist looks are common to all five.
  Each reel owns its signature device; nothing signature-level is shared.

## 8. What I cut, and why (beyond the concepts)

- Every "unsaved", "error" or "delete" dialog (his Yaadein signature). Cursor hesitation (Yaadein). Top/bottom splits
  (Genjutsu). Spotlight clunk cold opens as a hook (his "You made it" reel).
- Synthesised crowds, cheers, whispers that pretend to be words, and any second human voice. Absence is cheaper and
  stronger than a fake.
- A second floodlight clunk, a second orbit, a second underline wipe in any reel. Each device is spent once.
- Hours, rates, client names, view counts, "award" language. The numbers on screen are the reel's own (layers,
  frames, 0.03 s).
