01
Executive summary

Premium look, local humour, and tax info people pass on

RKN already has a strong brand promise sitting quietly on its own website: “Not Your Everyday Accountant.” The identity is premium (black and gold, a monogram logo), but its Google reviews keep describing the team as “professional, qualified and down to earth” and say they “took lots of pressure off” clients. That gap between a luxury look and a friendly Bury team is the creative engine. Every video should look like a premium brand and sound like a Bury local.

“Accountants are boring”: we’re here to prove that wrong, one video at a time.

The strategy in one page

1 · Make it useful enough to save

Short, dated, one-point tax tips tied to real deadlines (Budget 28 Oct, the paper return deadline on 31 Oct, the MTD quarterly update on 7 Nov, the online deadline on 31 Jan). Useful content gets saved and sent, and sends and saves are the signals the platforms push hardest.

2 · Make it fun enough to share

Skits on universal pain points (the receipts shoebox, the brown envelope, “I’ll send it tomorrow”), reaction videos that bust viral tax myths, and trend remixes. People share comedy that makes their friends feel seen.

3 · Make it local enough to belong

Bury Market quizzes, Bury business spotlights, town-centre B-roll. Local content gets shared by local people and featured businesses, and it pulls in the clients RKN actually wants: owners within driving distance of Millett House.

4 · Use AI for “wow”, never for trust

Use Higgsfield for spectacle: filling an empty reception, receipts swirling into a golden tornado, horror atmosphere, trailer grades. Keep the trust elements real: your team, real reviews (with permission) and real advice. Label realistic AI content.

The shoot-day golden rule for AI

For every scene you plan to “add people to” with AI, film it three ways:

  1. Clean plate: 15 seconds of the empty space on a locked-off tripod with exposure locked. This is for frame-first editing and video-edit models.
  2. Stand-in take: the same framing with a team member doing the action (welcoming, handing over a coffee, walking in). Genjutsu works best when it can swap something already in the shot, so a stand-in gives it a person to replace, and you get natural motion and shadows for free.
  3. Moving version: a slow gimbal push-in of the empty space, which you can edit with Seedance 2.5’s video-edit mode or Kling 3.0 Omni Edit.

Also take reference stills of every person and prop (front, side, back). Genjutsu accepts up to 30 reference images per generation.

Algorithm signals to design for (2026)

Watch time and completion

Instagram ranks on predicted watch time and rewatches. TikTok leans heavily on completion rate. Short, tight edits with a loop back to the start help on both.

Sends and saves beat likes

DM shares (“sends”) are widely reported as Instagram’s strongest signal, and likes have been down-weighted. Design for “send this to…” moments.

Original and native

Reels with another platform’s watermark lose reach, and Reels over 3 minutes aren’t recommended to non-followers. Export clean files for each platform.

Third-party ranking-signal claims vary. Treat specific percentages as unverified, and treat your own Insights as the final word.

Tone of voice (premium, but never stiff)

On-screen hook
“Every business in Bury walks through this door eventually…”
Alternative

Beats

  1. 0–2s: empty reception, close-up of the clock going 8:59 → 9:00 (tick plus door chime).
  2. 2–10s: quick cuts as each new person “appears” with a whoosh and a gold sector label.
  3. 10–15s: a real team member greets and shakes hands (the human anchor).
  4. 15–18s: wide shot of the full room, everyone turns to camera and smiles.
  5. 18–20s: logo end card, then loop back to the empty room.

Shoot today

  • Tripod, locked exposure and focus: 15s empty plates in wide, desk-medium and door angles
  • The same three angles with team stand-ins doing the welcome or handshake
  • A slow gimbal push-in through the empty reception
  • Clock close-up, door opening, door-chime audio
  • Reference stills of the hi-vis vest, hard hat, chef whites, keys and ring light
  • Clean logo-wall close-up for the end card

AI step: Higgsfield

  • Most control (frame-first): export a frame from the clean plate. Use Nano Banana Pro (or Nano Banana 2 with an inpaint mask) to add each person. Then animate with Kling 3.0, using the empty frame as the start and the filled frame as the end, so people “materialise” while the room stays put.
  • Keep your real camera move: run the push-in plate through Seedance 2.5 (Video Edit mode) or Kling 3.0 Omni Edit with wardrobe reference images.
  • Using the stand-in takes: Genjutsu Object Swap (4–30s clip, up to 30 references, up to 1080p) recasts your stand-ins as the builder, chef and so on, while keeping their real motion and shadows.
Using this exact photo of our reception, add a smiling builder in a hi-vis vest and white hard hat standing at the front desk, being welcomed. Keep the room, furniture, lighting, camera angle, logo and all signage exactly the same. Photorealistic, natural skin, realistic floor shadows.

Compliance & brand safety

Stay trusted while you go viral

AI content
  • Label realistic AI using TikTok’s “AI-generated content” toggle. Meta applies an “AI info” label and detects C2PA content credentials.
  • Never create AI likenesses of real public figures (e.g. the Chancellor) or private individuals. TikTok bans unlabelled deepfakes of real people.
  • Get written consent from every team member whose likeness is used in AI.
  • Higgsfield’s own guidance: use footage you own or have permission for, with consent from anyone shown.
Testimonials & claims
  • No fake reviews or AI “clients”. The UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 bans fake reviews and misleading presentation of reviews.
  • Quote real reviews word-for-word, with permission.
  • Label dramatisations as “Dramatisation.”
  • No guaranteed savings or refund claims, in line with advertising rules (CAP Code).
Tax accuracy
  • A qualified team member signs off every tax claim before posting.
  • Date-stamp tax content (“Correct as of Oct 2026”).
  • Add “General information, not personal advice.”
  • Follow the conduct rules of whichever professional body RKN’s accountants belong to.
  • Never use HMRC logos or imply HMRC endorsement.
Privacy & rights
  • GDPR: no client documents, names, figures or screens in shot. Use dummy data.
  • Release forms for everyone featured. Avoid filming children in public.
  • Music: business accounts use the TikTok Commercial Music Library or Meta’s licensed sounds, not any trending song.
  • Stitch or Duet natively; never re-upload other creators’ videos.
