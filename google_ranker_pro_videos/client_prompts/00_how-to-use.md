## How to generate

1. **Make six character sheets, one per client person**, from their own photos with their permission (these videos say they are that client). Plain everyday clothes with no logos, in the outfit the prompts expect:
   - Golden Line Taxis owner: a dark quilted jacket over a plain grey fleece
   - Coventry Airport Taxis 247 owner: a plain navy jumper with the sleeves pushed up
   - Leam Taxis owner: a dark fleece zipped to the chin
   - York Cars manager: a dark work jacket over a plain sweatshirt, a radio earpiece, a lanyard with the card turned round
   - Organic Fostering director: a soft grey knit cardigan over a plain top, a lanyard with the card turned round
   - Zentro owner: a plain navy or charcoal work top
2. **Higgsfield settings for every prompt:** Seedance 2.5 · Reference mode · the ratio and seconds shown above the prompt · **1080p (set it; it can default lower)** · Audio on. If you use the selectors: Era 2020s, Physics realistic, Genre documentary or unset, Lens 24 mm for phone-filmed prompts and 50 mm for camera-filmed ones.
3. **Attach the character sheet so it is @Image 1**, paste the prompt exactly as written, and generate. Video 4 needs no attachment.
4. **Voice:** the client's lines are inside each prompt, with the second each one starts. Seedance makes the voice. To keep one voice per client across prompts (video 1's three parts; Coventry in videos 2, 4 and 8; Leam in videos 3 and 9): once you have a take you like, cut 5-10 seconds of its audio, attach it as @Audio 1 in that client's next prompt, and add this line at the end of the ASSET MAP: `@Audio 1: voice reference for the owner's voice only; ignore its background sound.` If the client sends a voice memo of their own voice, use that as @Audio 1 instead.
5. **The lines are draft wording.** Send each client their lines first. If they change a line, change it in the prompt (same start second, similar length) and in the script table.
6. **If a clip looks AI, plastic or like a game render,** change one thing at a time: remake the character sheet from daylight phone photos (a glossy sheet gives a glossy video); regenerate; if one shot inside a prompt keeps failing, delete that shot from the prompt, shorten the seconds, and generate it on its own.
