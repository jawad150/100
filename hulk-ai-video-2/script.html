Hulk Marketing: AI Video Concept

"Run It Again" · 28 seconds · AI-generated live action (Seedance or Kling AI) · 9:16 · Lead generation ad

The Idea

A customer with an emergency is ready to pay a local business right now. We follow her search in real time: she finds the business, its website is too slow, she taps back and books the competitor whose ad was sitting right there. The business owner never even knows she existed. Then the video rewinds and plays the same moment again, this time with Hulk's marketing in place. Same customer, different ending.

The rewind is the creative device. It shows, in one simple story, exactly where businesses lose customers (search, website, ads) and what changes when those are fixed. It ends with one clear offer: a free audit to find out how many customers you're missing.

The Hook (first 2 seconds)

On-screen text: This customer was ready to pay you. Watch what happened.

Alternative hooks to A/B test:

  • "You lost a customer today. You just don't know it yet."
  • "She searched for you. She found your competitor."

Who It's For

  • UK small and medium business owners who rely on local customers: trades, clinics, salons, cafés, retail and service businesses.
  • People who feel busy but suspect they are missing enquiries online, and who have never had their marketing checked.

Why It Generates Leads

  • Loss, not features: the viewer imagines their own lost customer, which is a stronger trigger than a list of services.
  • It covers three Hulk services in one story without listing them: SEO and Google Maps, website, and paid ads.
  • It ends with a low-risk, specific offer (a free audit) and a direct question: "How many customers are you missing?"

Characters

  • The Customer: a woman in her 30s at home, casual clothes, stressed, with water leaking from under her kitchen sink.
  • The Owner: a plumber in his 40s, work jacket, sitting in his van with his phone on the dashboard. He stands in for any local business owner.

Create one reference image of each character first and use it in every shot so they look the same throughout.

Look

  • Realistic, cinematic UK setting: a normal home kitchen and a work van on a residential street.
  • First half (the lost customer): cool, slightly blue grade, tense.
  • Replay (with Hulk): warmer, brighter grade, with a Hulk green (#9DEF69) accent on the on-screen labels.
  • Phone screens (search results, website, call button) are added in editing, not generated by AI, so text and logos stay sharp and correct.

Shot by Shot

Shot 1 · 0:00–0:02 · Hook

Visual: Close-up of the customer's face lit by her phone, water dripping behind her, wet kitchen floor.

On-screen text: This customer was ready to pay you. Watch what happened.

Sound: Dripping water, a ticking clock.

Shot 2 · 0:02–0:05 · The search

Visual: Over-the-shoulder shot of her thumb typing on her phone. In editing, the screen shows: "emergency plumber near me".

On-screen text: She searched for a plumber.

Sound: Keyboard taps.

Shot 3 · 0:05–0:08 · The slow website

Visual: She taps a result ("your" business). The screen shows a loading page that doesn't load. She sighs, shakes her head and taps back.

On-screen text: Your website was too slow. She didn't wait.

Sound: A long, flat loading tone.

Shot 4 · 0:08–0:11 · The competitor

Visual: The screen shows a competitor's ad at the top. She taps "Call", puts the phone to her ear and looks relieved.

On-screen text: Your competitor's ad was right there.

Sound: Phone dialling, then "Hello, yes, I can come now."

Shot 5 · 0:11–0:14 · The owner

Visual: The plumber sits in his van, his phone silent on the dashboard. He checks it, nothing. He looks out of the window.

On-screen text: You never even knew she existed.

Sound: Quiet van interior, distant traffic. The music drops out.

Shot 6 · 0:14–0:16 · Rewind

Visual: Everything rewinds fast: the call ends, the competitor's ad disappears, she is back at the search, the water drips upwards.

On-screen text: Let's run that again.

Sound: A tape-rewind sound, then the music kicks in.

Shot 7 · 0:16–0:21 · The replay, with Hulk

Visual: The same search, faster cuts, warmer light. This time "your" business is first in the results and on the map; the website opens instantly; she taps "Call now" and smiles.

On-screen labels (one per beat, in green): Found first · Loads instantly · The right ad at the right moment

Sound: Three crisp clicks on the beat.

Shot 8 · 0:21–0:24 · The phone rings

Visual: In the van, the phone lights up and rings. The plumber answers with a grin, starts the engine and pulls away.

On-screen text: Same customer. Different ending.

Sound: Phone ring, engine start.

AI Prompts (Seedance or Kling AI)

Generate each shot as a separate 5-second clip from the character reference images (image-to-video). Leave phone screens blank; the screen content is added in editing.

  1. Hook: Close-up of a stressed woman in her 30s in a UK home kitchen, face lit by her phone screen, water dripping from under the sink behind her, wet floor, cool blue light, realistic, cinematic, shallow depth of field, 9:16.
  2. Search: Over-the-shoulder shot of the same woman typing quickly on her phone with her thumb, blank glowing screen, kitchen in the background, handheld, realistic.
  3. Slow website: The same woman staring at her phone waiting, then sighing in frustration and tapping the screen, impatient expression, cool light, realistic.
  4. Competitor: The same woman puts her phone to her ear, her expression changing to relief as someone answers, kitchen background, realistic.
  5. Owner: A plumber in his 40s in a work jacket sitting in the driver's seat of a white van on a UK residential street, a silent phone on the dashboard, he checks it and looks out of the window, overcast light, realistic, cinematic.
  6. Rewind: Use clips 1–4 reversed in the editor (no separate generation needed).
  7. Replay: The same woman in the same kitchen, warm golden light, tapping her phone and smiling as she lifts it to her ear, realistic, cinematic.
  8. Phone rings: The same plumber in the same van, his phone on the dashboard lights up, he answers with a big smile and starts the engine, warm daylight, realistic.

Negative prompt: cartoon, text, logos, distorted hands, extra fingers, warped phone, blurry faces.

Production Notes

  • Seedance and Kling work best with one simple action per clip; keep each shot to a single movement.
  • Build the phone screens (search results, slow loading page, ads, call button) as simple mock-ups and track them onto the phone in the editor (CapCut, Premiere or After Effects). Use a generic made-up business name, not a real competitor.
  • The rewind is done in editing: reverse shots 1–4 at high speed and add a tape-rewind sound.
  • Captions on screen throughout; most people watch without sound.
  • Cutdown: a 15-second version can run Shots 1, 3, 5, 6, 7 and 9.
