# Concept B: "Every call is a fare" (story-first, human-led)

Pitch for the full 15-video plan for Google Ranker Pro / Hulk Marketing. Creative director B. Written 22 Oct 2026. British English.

Source of truth: every research file in this folder, `production_rules.md` above all, and the screenshots in `../assets/`. The user's realism brief governs every line: *"it must not feel like ai, cgi or plastic effect or 3d game render it must look like a real life shot, videos."*

---

## 0. The pitch in one paragraph

Local search is about the moment a stranger who needs a taxi at four in the morning finds one, rather than about rankings, and a small business owner can stop worrying that the phone won't ring. So we film owners, drivers and the agency team as real people in real places: a car parked in Warwick before sunrise, a kitchen table in Coventry after the last shift, a dispatch room in York on a Saturday night, a desk by a rainy window. Each client film runs **problem → turn → result**, told in the owner's own words and voice. The data (Search Console and Business Profile, always real and always cited) arrives at about 70% of the runtime as the **payoff**. It is never the opening slide, except on social hooks where a number earns the first second. The look is warm observational documentary: handheld, available light, wet tarmac, steamed-up windows and room tone. The recurring image is **a phone lighting up**, face-up on a dashboard or desk, its screen never readable. That is what the agency sells.

Why this angle wins on realism: documentary grammar is the grammar audiences already trust. It prefers imperfect light, small gestures, faces at rest and real ambience, and those are the qualities that stop AI footage looking like AI. Story-first also makes the honest version stronger: an owner's **real voice memo** over faceless footage of real-looking work is more believable than any polished presenter.

## 1. Story rules for all 15 videos

1. **Problem → turn → result, in that order.** The problem is human: missed early runs, a quiet phone, nobody finding the firm. The turn is the work, stated only as the agency's real work log records it. The result is real data, sourced on screen.
2. **Data is the payoff, not the wallpaper.** In web pieces (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 10, 13, 15) the first number lands at roughly 65–75% of the runtime. On social pieces (3, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 14) one real number or query may open the video in the first second (autoplay search), and the human beat then explains it.
3. **Owners speak only their own words.** For client videos we send interview questions, record the answers as voice memos, transcribe them and get the transcript approved. Only then do those exact words go into Seedance as dialogue. Any "draft line" in this document is a suggestion for the person to approve or rewrite. Each one is built only from verified facts.
4. **One place per scene, one time of day per scene.** Story lives in place. Every video names its town, and the town is real.
5. **The phone motif.** Every client strand contains one shot of a phone lighting up face-up, screen dark or green, never readable. It ties the 15 films together without a logo.
6. **Labels, always.**
   - On Mode B footage (a real person recreated from their own photos): *"AI-recreated with [Name]'s permission. Words and results are theirs."*
   - On the Mode A fallback presenter: *"AI presenter. Real client data."*
   - On faceless reconstructions: *"Dramatised visuals. Real voice, real data."*
   - On every number: *"Source · date range"*.
   - Set the platform AI labels as well (TikTok AIGC, YouTube altered or synthetic content, Meta AI info).
7. **Brand on screen.** Use "Hulk Marketing" where the user's plan says Hulk (UK social, client videos). On googlerankerpro.com placements, use either "Google Ranker Pro" or the user's chosen lockup (open question Q1).

## 2. World Bible (one real-life shoot, 15 films)

Paste these lines **verbatim** into every prompt of the set (production-consistency look lock). The set is one late-October shoot in the English Midlands, York and Manchester: autumn, damp, coats on, short days.

### 2.1 Palette line (verbatim in every prompt)
`Muted, lived-in colour: overcast grey-white sky, wet charcoal tarmac, navy, grey and olive clothing, warm tungsten and sodium-orange pockets of light, cool daylight at windows; nothing saturated.`

Brand colours (GRP orange `#F7931E` and blue `#3FA9F5`, Hulk lime `#C6FA61`, and every client colour) live **only in the edit's overlays**. They never appear in a generation, with one allowed exception: an incidental real-world object whose colour happens to echo the brand, such as a mustard scarf, which is never placed on purpose.

### 2.2 Light logic (one line per strand; verbatim across every shot in that strand)
- **L-TAXI (pre-dawn, dawn and night car work: 1, 3, 4, 5, 9, 13 b-roll):** `Blue-grey early daylight through the car windows mixed with the warm amber of street lamps outside; the dashboard stays dark; the face is lit softly from the side window and falls off into shadow on the far cheek; rain beads on the glass catch small points of light.`
- **L-HOME (client kitchens, back offices and desks after work: 1, 2, 4, 7):** `A warm tungsten ceiling bulb or lamp camera-left is the key; cool blue-grey dusk from a window camera-right; the two colour temperatures meet on the face; corners of the room fall into soft shadow.`
- **L-OFFICE (agency room and client offices by day: 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15):** the production-rules UK office daylight line, verbatim: `Soft overcast daylight from a large window camera-left is the only key; the right side of the face falls a stop darker with soft shadow under the cheekbone; the overhead office lights are on but weak, adding a faint cooler top light; the window itself is slightly overexposed.`
- **L-STREET (exteriors: Warwick, Leamington, Kenilworth, Coventry, York, Old Trafford):** `Flat overcast sky as a huge soft source from above; soft shadows under the eyes and chin; wet tarmac reflects the grey sky; no direct sun.` Blue-hour and night variants use L-TAXI.
- **Deep skin tones** always add: `exposed correctly for deep brown skin so the highlights show natural sheen and the shadow side keeps detail.`
- **Dark sets** always add: `a faint rim of window light separates the head and shoulders from the dark background.`

### 2.3 Texture lines (one capture mode per video; verbatim from production_rules D.1)
- **DOC-CAM** (spine of every web film: 1, 2, 4, 5 inserts, 6, 10, 13, 15)
- **PHONE-SELFIE** (3, 7, 9, 12 to-camera, 14)
- **PHONE-REAR** (5 walk-round, 8, 11, 12 b-roll)

In finishing, use **one grain value and one sharpen value per video**. Add no film-stock wording.

### 2.4 Motifs and set dressing (the "lived-in" layer)
- **Recurring objects:**
  - a phone face-up lighting up (screen dark or green);
  - a mug of tea that is actually drunk;
  - car keys on a fob;
  - a coat over a chair back;
  - rain on glass;
  - a radiator under a window;
  - a blank paper diary or booking pad, its pages turned away.
- **Every set gets** two or three dressing anchors that stay put, plus one uncomposed element: a half-cropped passer-by, a crooked blind, a cable across the floor, a parking ticket on the dash turned face-down.
- **Atmosphere between lens and subject:** breath in cold air, steam from a mug, drizzle, a smeared windscreen with the wiper parked mid-glass.
- **Behaviour:** people blink, look away to think, restart a gesture, rub the back of the neck, laugh at themselves and swallow before a line. Write gestures, never emotions.

### 2.5 Sound bible (generation side)
- Each strand has its own ambience:
  - **Taxi:** wipers, indicator tick, rain on the roof, a far-off lorry, dispatch radio crackle with **no intelligible words**.
  - **Office:** keyboard, radiator tick, kettle click, chair creak.
  - **Home:** fridge hum, clock, a dog two rooms away.
  - **Street:** tyres on wet road, a bus kneeling, footsteps.
- No music and no subtitles. Handles of 1 s of ambience at the head and tail. The edit adds one sparse licensed bed (piano or nylon guitar) mixed low, and none at all under testimonial answers.

### 2.6 Forbidden list (applies to every generation)
- Hype and polish tokens. Use none of these: cinematic, film look, 8K, ultra-realistic, photorealistic, flawless, glossy, studio, beauty, editorial, epic, masterpiece. Name no camera or lens brands, ISO or f-stops.
- Gimbal, steadicam, dolly, slider, crane, drone, slow motion or speed ramps.
- Creamy bokeh on phone-mode shots.
- Studio black-background portraits. The founders' existing studio clips are a different language and must not be copied.
- Readable text of any kind: signs, plates, mugs, clothing, paper, screens. No invented firm names, liveries, roof signs or licence badges. No Google logo or Google colours. No generated search results or Search Console, Business Profile or report screens; screens are angled away, defocused or flat green.
- Faces:
  - No face prose for any real person (Mode B).
  - No generated face standing in for a real client, and never a generated "Ali".
  - No identifiable children (Organic Fostering: children only from behind, as hands, or out of focus; in practice, none at all).
- Data:
  - No "No.1".
  - No growth percentages.
  - No Hulk portfolio percentage ranges (Leam +60–300%, Organic Fostering +58% / −70%).
  - No site self-claims (15,000+ customers, 4.6 from 100+ reviews, Zentro 4.8 from 2,560+).
  - No Global Cars 4.9 from 202 widget.
  - No Organic Fostering allowance figures. The old £447.60 set is superseded, so show no allowance figures at all.
  - No prices from the GRP pricing page.
- Weather and light:
  - No golden-hour glamour beyond one shot per video.
  - No sunny-blue-sky "advert" weather. October is grey.
- Presenter look: no look-alike of the five existing polished AI presenters. Avoid catalogue navy blazers, perfect teeth and tidy cream rooms.
