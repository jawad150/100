Hulk Marketing: Static Post Concept

"First Impression" · 1080 × 1350 (4:5) · Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn

What We Saw on Pinterest

  • Split-screen ads are a strong, popular format: one image cut down the middle into two versions of the same thing (ordinary vs. better, before vs. after, "your phone" vs. "our phone").
  • The best ones keep the same subject on both sides, so the only thing that changes is the difference the brand makes. The viewer gets it instantly.
  • The "your problems / our solution" layout also appears in agency ad boards, but usually as cluttered text. We can do it visually instead.

The Idea

A hand holds a phone showing a local business's online listing, split straight down the middle. On the left, the listing is what most small businesses look like online: no photos, no reviews, no website, dull and easy to scroll past. On the right, it's the same business after Hulk: a great shopfront photo, five-star reviews, "Open now" and a bright "Book now" button. Same business. Different first impression. Every owner instantly asks themselves: which side am I?

It speaks directly to Hulk's local services: SEO, Local Maps Marketing, Reputation Management and Website Design.

Visual

  • One real photo of a hand holding a smartphone upright, in the centre of the frame.
  • A clean vertical split line runs down the middle of the whole post, through the phone.
  • Left side (without Hulk): background in a flat, muted grey-beige; phone screen shows a plain listing with a grey empty photo box, "No reviews yet", "No website" and faded text.
  • Right side (with Hulk): background in forest green #111A16; phone screen shows the same business name with a warm shopfront photo, five gold stars, "Open now" in green and a green "Book now" button.
  • The phone screen is a simple, generic mock-up designed in Figma or Photoshop. Do not use Google's logo or interface; keep it brand-neutral.
  • No other props, icons or decoration.

Photo / AI Prompt

For the hand and phone, use a real photo or this AI prompt, then add the split background and screen designs in Photoshop:

"Close-up photo of a hand holding a modern smartphone upright in front of a plain background, phone screen blank and facing the camera, natural skin tones, soft studio light, sharp focus, centred, realistic, no text."

Layout (1080 × 1350)

  1. Headline at the top, across both halves: Inter Tight 900, about 90 px, two lines. Ink #20392E on the left half, cream #F4F2E9 on the right (the colour switches at the split line).
  2. Two small labels above the phone: "Ignored" on the left and "Chosen" on the right, Hanken Grotesk 600, 34 px, in caps.
  3. The hand and phone in the centre: the focal point.
  4. Supporting line under the phone: Hanken Grotesk 400, 34 px.
  5. CTA button on the right half: green #9DEF69 pill, forest text, Hanken Grotesk 600, 34 px.
  6. Logo, small, bottom right.

Rules: two fonts only, minimum text size 34 px, 80 px margins, no more than about 25 words on the post.

Colours

  • Muted grey-beige #D9D6CC: left half (the "ignored" side)
  • Forest #111A16: right half (the Hulk side)
  • Cream #F4F2E9 and ink #20392E: text
  • Hulk green #9DEF69: "Open now", the "Book now" button on the screen and the CTA button only

Copy

Headline: Same business. Different first impression.

Labels: Ignored / Chosen

Supporting line: Customers judge you online before they ever walk in.

CTA button: Get my free audit ↗

Alternative headlines:

  • Which one would you call?
  • Your customers already compared. Did you win?

Why It Works

  • Instant comparison: understood in one second, no reading needed.
  • Personal: every owner checks which side their own business is on.
  • One focal point, two fonts, large readable text and no decoration.
  • Conversion-focused: the free audit offer answers the question the post raises ("What do customers see when they find me?").

Other Sizes

  • Stories (1080 × 1920): same split; headline at the top, phone in the middle, button above the bottom 340 px, plus a link sticker.
  • LinkedIn (1:1): crop tighter on the phone; keep the headline and button.
  • Carousel option: slide 1 shows only the left half ("Is this you?"), slide 2 reveals the full split.
